# 3. The 5-minute stage pitch

**Title: The Trust Layer for African Sport — in the AI Era**  
**Target length:** Approximately 750–800 words

### [0:00–0:40] The hook

In one week, the world will turn its eyes to Senegal for the JOJ Dakar 2026. But behind every major sporting event is something most people never see: the technology and data that keep everything moving.

Good morning, everyone. My name is Abdoullah Ndao, and I present to you **Ultorn**.

We are building a cybersecurity platform in Dakar to help African organizations protect their digital infrastructure in the age of AI. And the JOJ present an opportunity to think about something bigger than one sporting event: how we protect the future of African sport.

### [0:40–1:40] The vibe-coding problem

Think about everything involved in organizing the JOJ. Athletes, venues, accreditation, ticketing, communications, partners and spectators all depend on an enormous amount of coordination.

Now think about what happens when digital systems become part of that equation.

AI has made building software incredibly easy. A small team can now create an application in a weekend that would once have required weeks of development.

That is a genuine opportunity for African sport. But there is a problem.

The person building that application might not know how to secure it. AI can generate code with exposed passwords, unsafe configurations, or administrative access that should never be public.

And the risk doesn't stop at software. Organizations handle athlete records, medical information, data belonging to minors, and fans' personal details. Staff may even send sensitive information to external AI models without realizing what they are exposing.

The result? We are building digital services faster than many organizations can secure them.

And when something goes wrong during a major sporting event, the consequences extend far beyond the IT department.

### [1:40–2:20] The race we can win

Now, Senegal is not going to out-build OpenAI, Google, or China in the foundation-model race. And we don't need to.

The opportunity is to build the technology that protects the systems, information, and institutions using AI.

Because if every security tool we rely on is developed and hosted elsewhere, we risk becoming dependent on infrastructure we do not control.

The JOJ are a historic milestone for Senegal. But the opportunity goes beyond hosting a successful event. We can also build local expertise and technology that continue creating value for African sport long after the Games end.

**We may not win the race to build the biggest AI model. But we can build the security layer that African organizations need around it.**

That is the race Ultorn is entering.

### [2:20–3:30] The solution

Ultorn is a defensive cybersecurity platform built by Utachi Industries in Dakar. It does five things.

**First, it finds exposure.** We scan authorized domains, websites, and infrastructure to identify vulnerabilities and insecure configurations before they become bigger problems.

**Second, it monitors.** We can continuously check important services for outages, configuration changes, and other security issues. For a sports organization, that might mean its website, ticketing platform, membership system, or streaming service.

**Third, it creates accountability.** Findings are documented, reviewed, and tracked. Critical issues require human review, and the audit trail helps organizations understand what happened and what action was taken.

**Fourth, it helps with compliance.** We map security findings to relevant obligations and turn technical results into reports that management can understand. A security issue should lead to a clear decision, not just another complicated dashboard.

**Fifth, it protects AI workflows.** Our AI Guard is designed to remove sensitive information before prompts reach external models, while local models offer another option for organizations that need tighter control. Our code scanner also looks for common mistakes, such as exposed credentials and unsafe code.

These are not five separate products. They are parts of one platform designed to help organizations find risks, stay informed, and respond properly.

### [3:30–4:20] The sports application

Now, imagine applying this across African sport.

A club gets its website, ticketing platform, and fan app assessed before the season. A federation gets a consistent way to assess its affiliated clubs. An academy gets better visibility into how it protects young athletes' information.

During matchday, critical services can be monitored. Afterwards, the organization has a record of the issues identified and the steps taken to address them.

The JOJ and the work of the COJOJ bring the importance of sports infrastructure into focus. But the opportunity does not end with Dakar 2026. Clubs, academies, federations, and future sporting events will continue to need digital systems they can trust.

This is where Africa Rise comes in.

Africa Rise understands the sporting ecosystem and the people within it. We bring the cybersecurity technology. Together, we can make security more accessible to organizations that may not have their own dedicated cybersecurity teams.

And we can help build a Senegalese technology business that serves African sport rather than leaving the market entirely to imported solutions.

### [4:20–4:45] Proof and model

Ultorn is already a working platform, with scanning, monitoring, reporting, code security checks, AI protection, and an operator console. Our current build has 88 passing backend tests, and we have a sports demonstration environment.

Our business model combines initial security assessments, monthly monitoring, and larger agreements with federations and leagues. The next step is to validate the product with real clients and convert those pilots into long-term partnerships.

### [4:45–5:00] The ask and close

We are looking for **three pilot clubs or federations in the next 90 days**, with Africa Rise as our go-to-market partner.

The JOJ will be a landmark for African sport. Let's use this moment to build more than a sporting legacy. Let's build the African technology and expertise that will protect what comes next.

My name is Abdoullah Ndao. This is Ultorn.

**Know before they do. Thank you.**

## 4. Delivery notes

- **The hook:** Mention the JOJ first, then introduce yourself. The audience should understand immediately why the subject matters to Senegal now.
- **“My name is Abdoullah Ndao, and I present to you Ultorn.”** Deliver this confidently. This is your introduction, not a slogan.
- **“That is a genuine opportunity for African sport.”** Keep your tone positive. You are not against AI; you are addressing the risks that come with faster development.
- **“We may not win the race…”** Slow down. This is the central argument connecting AI, cybersecurity, and digital sovereignty.
- **The five features:** Make them understandable to a non-technical audience. Do not rush through tool names or implementation details.
- **The COJOJ and JOJ:** Use the Games as the context for the opportunity. Do not imply that Ultorn is deployed by the COJOJ, that the committee has a cybersecurity problem, or that you have an existing partnership unless that is confirmed.
- **The ask:** Be direct about three pilots and Africa Rise as your go-to-market partner. Do not dilute the closing with several additional requests.
- **The ending:** Finish with “Know before they do.” Then “Thank you.” Nothing else.
