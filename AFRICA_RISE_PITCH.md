# The Trust Layer for African Sport — in the AI Era

**A 5-minute pitch for Africa Rise**
**Audience:** Africa Rise leadership and partners
**Goal:** three pilot clubs/federations + a go-to-market partnership
**Length:** ~750 words, about 5 minutes at a measured pace

---

## [0:00–0:40] The hook

Imagine it's matchday in Dakar. The stadium is full. The club has just signed the biggest sponsorship deal in its history. And at two o'clock, the ticketing platform goes down.

Not because of traffic. Because someone vibe-coded the new ticketing app three weeks ago, pasted an API key into the frontend, and shipped it. Nobody was watching.

That is the new risk in African sport. Not just hackers. Speed. The AI era is arriving faster than the security layer around it.

## [0:40–1:40] The vibe-coding problem

Let's be honest about what's happening. The next generation of sports software in Africa will not be built by large engineering teams. It will be built by a club's marketing lead, a federation's operations manager, a founder with an idea and a prompt.

That is a superpower. A club in Dakar can have a ticketing platform, a fan app, an academy portal, and a scouting tool in a weekend.

But AI writes code that looks right. It hardcodes keys to make the demo work. It disables TLS when the certificate fails. It trusts user input. It ships `eval`, opens CORS to the world, and leaves the admin panel on the public internet.

The code ships fast. The doors stay open.

And it's not just code. Staff are pasting athlete passports, medical notes, minors' records, and fan payment data into foreign AI models to write reports, summarize contracts, or draft communications. The data leaves the building. Nobody logs it. Nobody knows where it went.

## [1:40–2:20] The sovereignty problem

That's the second shift: sovereignty.

For decades, African institutions have had to choose between modern tools and control of their own data. The AI era makes that choice sharper. Every prompt sent to a foreign model is a small export of sovereignty. Every athlete record stored on someone else's cloud is trust you don't control.

Senegal's 2026 Cybersecurity Law now makes this concrete: incident reporting within 72 hours, local hosting for public-sector data, regular audits, and human oversight of AI. Sponsors, club licensing, and donor audits will ask for evidence — not assurances.

Sovereignty isn't a slogan. It's the difference between building on infrastructure you control and renting your trust from someone else.

## [2:20–3:30] The solution

Ultorn is a sovereign, defensive cybersecurity operations platform built in Dakar, for African institutions. It does five things.

**It finds exposure.** We scan domains, IPs, and URLs with Nuclei, Nmap, and full HTTP evidence collection. No exploitation. Just the truth about what's visible.

**It watches.** Continuous monitors check ticketing, streaming, membership, and results endpoints for outages, configuration drift, leaked credentials, and policy violations. When something changes, the right people get an alert.

**It proves.** Every scan, finding, review, and action goes into an immutable audit log. Critical findings require a named analyst to sign off. When a sponsor, federation, or auditor asks what happened, you have the record.

**It complies.** Findings map to Senegal Cyber Law 2026 — CERT windows, audit obligations, AI governance — and become a plain-language report a board can actually read.

**And it protects the AI era in two directions.** AI Guard strips client names, IPs, credentials, emails, phone numbers, and card data before anything reaches an external model — or runs the whole thing on a local open-source model, so no foreign vendor owns your workflow. And our code scanner catches the fingerprints of vibe coding — hardcoded secrets, unsafe `eval`, disabled TLS, insecure defaults — before they reach fans.

## [3:30–4:20] The sports application

Now put that in the sports context.

A **club** gets its website, ticketing, academy, sponsor microsites, and fan app assessed before the season — then monitored through matchday.

A **federation or league** gets every club as a client, one governance view, and one evidence trail for licensing and sponsors.

An **academy** gets athlete and minor records protected, access logged, and AI-assisted reports that never leak a child's identity.

A **stadium or broadcaster** gets ticketing, streaming, and results watched from 24 hours before kickoff to six hours after.

Africa Rise is building the network, the credibility, and the narrative of African sport. Utachi builds products from Dakar for this market. Together, we don't have to import trust. We can build it here.

## [4:20–4:45] Proof and model

We have the platform working today: client onboarding, scanning, continuous monitoring, findings and human review, compliance mapping, executive reports, AI Guard, code scanning, audit, and admin. Eighty-eight backend tests. A working console. And a sports demo tenant — Dakar United FC — with real ticketing, academy, stadium, and fan-app findings.

The model is a baseline assessment per club, a monthly monitoring subscription, a sovereign-AI tier for academies and federations, and a league-wide enterprise agreement. Africa Rise can co-brand it, distribute it, and share the revenue.

## [4:45–5:00] The ask and close

We're looking for three pilot clubs or federations in the next 90 days, and Africa Rise as our go-to-market partner.

Africa Rise builds the future of African sport. Ultorn makes sure that when it gets there — in the AI era — the data, the money, and the kids are safe.

Know before they do. Thank you.

## Delivery notes

- **Pause after "nobody was watching."** Then deliver "The AI era is arriving faster than the security layer around it" slowly — it sets up everything that follows.
- **"That is a superpower."** Change your tone here. You're not anti-AI. You're pro-AI-with-a-safety-net. That distinction matters to this audience.
- **"The code ships fast. The doors stay open."** Beat. Let the room sit with it.
- **Slow down on the data list:** passports, medical notes, minors' records, fan payment data.
- **"We can build it here."** Stop. Look at the room. That's the partnership moment.
- **The ask** — "three pilots in 90 days" — should be the clearest sentence in the pitch.
- End on "Know before they do." Then "Thank you." Nothing else.
